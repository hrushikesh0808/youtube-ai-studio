**(Intro Music - Ethereal, slightly unsettling piano melody fades in and then under)**

Hey everyone, and welcome back to ‘Echoes of the Past’. Tonight’s story isn't just about a place; it's about lost dreams, forgotten lives, and a silence so deep you can almost hear it breathing. I want to tell you about Oakhaven… a village that vanished from maps, swallowed by the Blackwood Forest, and left behind only whispers and a lingering sadness.  I found this story through old journals – the handwriting faded, the ink bleeding like tears – and believe me, after spending days in those woods, I started to understand why.

**(Transition - Sound of wind rustling through leaves, distant birdsong)**

The legend of Oakhaven began about a hundred years ago. It was settled by a group of families fleeing religious persecution. They sought refuge amongst the ancient trees, hoping for peace and a new start.  Old Man Hemlock’s journal paints such a vivid picture – his wife, Elara, tending her herb garden, their children laughing as they chased butterflies, the blacksmith hammering rhythmically in the evenings. There was a sense of simple joy there, desperately clinging to hope. But something shifted. Slowly, subtly, the entries became… darker. The laughter faded. The descriptions of the forest changed from serene beauty to an oppressive presence. 

**(Visual - Gentle slow-motion shot of moss growing on a weathered wooden sign reading "Oakhaven")**

Locating Oakhaven wasn’t easy. The Blackwood Forest is vast and dense, notoriously disorienting. Locals warned me against it – “Don't go seeking what’s lost,” they said. But the journals... they pulled at me. After three days of following barely discernible trails, I found it.  It was like stepping back in time. The houses were untouched; furniture still in place as if the inhabitants had just stepped out for a moment. A half-finished loaf of bread sat on a table. Children’s toys lay scattered across the dirt. It wasn't just abandoned – it felt *left*.

**(Sound - Adds subtle creaking wood, distant raven call)** 

The air itself felt heavy with sorrow. I spent hours wandering through the village, documenting everything. In the schoolhouse, a child’s drawing of a smiling sun lay on the floor; in the church, a hymnal was open to a page filled with handwritten notations – prayers unanswered.  Then, I found Elara’s garden. It was overgrown, choked by weeds, but beneath the brambles, I unearthed something chilling. A small wooden box, intricately carved. Inside, a single, perfectly preserved white rose. And a note, penned in Elara’s elegant script: “They’re taking them.” 

**(Music - Piano becomes more urgent, dissonant chords added)**

That's when the fear truly set in. I started hearing things – whispers on the wind, rustling just beyond my vision.  The journal entries from later days spoke of a growing unease, of shadows moving amongst the trees, of unsettling silence broken only by children crying. One entry, written frantically just days before the village vanished completely, simply stated: “They’re here. They want what we built.” 

**(Visual - Quick cut to a blurred shot of something dark moving through the trees)** 

I didn't know *who* "they" were, but I knew I had to leave. As I turned back towards the trail, I noticed something carved into the door of the blacksmith’s shop – a symbol: a spiral within a circle. It was identical to one I'd seen repeatedly in the drawings left by the children.  It felt… ancient.  

**(Transition - Sound of wind intensifying)**

I didn't stop running until I burst out of the Blackwood Forest, leaving Oakhaven behind, swallowed once more by its silent embrace. Back at my campsite, pouring over the final entries in Old Man Hemlock’s journal, I discovered the horrifying truth. Oakhaven hadn’t been destroyed – it had *chosen* to disappear. The villagers hadn't fled persecution; they’d made a pact with something within the forest, an entity that fed on memories and emotions. They offered themselves willingly in exchange for peace… a peace that ultimately consumed them.  The spiral symbol was a key, a gateway. 

**(Music - Piano melody returns to its initial ethereal state)** 

Oakhaven wasn't just abandoned; it was integrated into the forest’s consciousness. And perhaps, somewhere within those silent houses and overgrown gardens, a piece of their souls still lingers, waiting for someone foolish enough to seek them out.  I don't know if I’ve truly escaped Oakhaven’s influence – sometimes, when I close my eyes, I can almost hear the children laughing… or crying. 

**(Outro Music - Piano melody fades up and then out completely)**
